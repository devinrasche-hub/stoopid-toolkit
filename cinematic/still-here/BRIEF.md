# The brief: STILL HERE (STOOPID AFTER HOURS 03)

This is the prompt that produced the film, kept word for word for the next installment.

Its shape is new. It locks three blanks before any visual is designed (the one idea, the flip, and a real-life anchor), and every beat answers to them. Reuse that opening for the next film (see `../PLAYBOOK.md`).

---

Build the third JavaScript cinematic for THE STOOPID SHOW’s “STOOPID AFTER HOURS” series.
Before designing any visuals, lock these three blanks:
THE ONE IDEA
Your family does not need an audience to notice your absence.
THE FLIP
The station presents leaving as disappearing; outside it, someone has been waiting for you to come home.
THE FAMILY OR REAL-LIFE ANCHOR
A father’s ordinary bedtime ritual with his child: sitting beside the bed for one more minute. The child’s exact line is “You forgot our minute.” This is a fictional scene grounded in Dev’s life as a father, not a claimed family quote.
Every creative choice must serve those three answers.
TITLE: STILL HERE
INSTALLMENT: 03
DURATION: Exactly 120 seconds.
FORMAT: 16:9, 1920×1080 target.
MEDIUM: JavaScript cinematic with Three.js and carefully timed sound.
Inspect the existing repository. Preserve the first two films. Add this as a separately playable sequel using the established renderer, timeline, controls, audio system, and capture pipeline where possible.
CONTINUITY
Film 01, PLEASE REMAIN SEEN:
An entity survived by being watched. Its last request was “Stay?”
Film 02, PLEASE HOLD:
The entity helped the viewer shut down the station. An exit opened onto apparent morning. The final title card revealed:
“AUTOPLAY STARTING…”
Film 03 begins on that exact message.
This time, the station tries to keep the viewer by impersonating home.
The viewer escapes by recognizing a small, specific family ritual that the station cannot reduce to a performance.
The protagonist is a father represented through a first-person camera. No realistic animated child model is required. Convey family through room details, small movements, sound, and carefully placed dialogue.
EXACT STORYBOARD
0–8 SECONDS — AUTOMATIC RETURN
Picture begins on the previous black title card.
Exact on-screen text:
“AUTOPLAY STARTING…”
At 2 seconds, add:
“UP NEXT: STILL HERE”
At 4 seconds, the camera begins pulling back. Reveal that this card is playing on a CRT in an otherwise dark room.
At 6 seconds, add:
“SELECTED FOR YOU”
One familiar relay click. Faint electrical hum.
No narration.
8–20 SECONDS — HOME, APPARENTLY
Continue pulling back into a modest living room.
A couch.
A tipped-over toy truck.
Small shoes beside adult work shoes.
A mug with a dried coffee ring.
Warm light spilling from a hallway.
Keep it lived-in, modest, believable.
At 11 seconds, a caption appears:
“DAD?”
Use a prerecorded child voice only if an appropriate local recording is available. Otherwise, let the caption and sound design carry the moment. Do not generate fake family recordings or use browser speech synthesis.
Camera stops moving for two seconds.
Then it turns slowly toward the hallway.
At 16 seconds, the CRT behind us displays:
“WE KNEW YOU’D LIKE THIS.”
Make this readable near the edge of the composition as the camera turns.
The warm room tone contains a nearly imperceptible version of the station’s hold melody.
20–33 SECONDS — SOMETHING TOO PERFECT
Camera moves down the hallway at a cautious walking pace.
Pass three framed pictures.
Each contains the same empty couch from a slightly different angle. No family photographs.
At 24 seconds, The Algorithm’s caption:
“It has learned what makes you stay.”
At 28 seconds, the camera reaches a child’s bedroom doorway.
Inside:
A small bed.
A storybook.
A bedside chair.
A warm night-light.
The chair faces a camera mounted above the bed.
At 30 seconds, its red recording light switches on.
Camera slowly tilts up to notice it.
33–45 SECONDS — THE ASSIGNMENT
Cut to a close shot of the storybook lying open.
Exact printed words on the left page:
“YOUR BEST MOMENT”
Exact printed words on the right page:
“TAKE 01”
Camera slides sideways, revealing a small amber tally display beside the bed.
Exact display text at 37 seconds:
“BE PRESENT.”
At 40 seconds, replace it with:
“LOOK NATURAL.”
At 42 seconds, replace it with:
“AGAIN.”
Three dry equipment clicks, one per instruction.
No joke sting. Let the absurdity feel invasive.
45–58 SECONDS — THE REAL SIGNAL
At 45 seconds, all electrical hum cuts out.
Three quiet knocks come from behind a plain wall with no door.
Camera turns toward the sound.
At 48 seconds, a small caption:
“Dad?”
This caption uses clean, stable typography. The station’s text has subtle raster instability; this line does not.
At 51 seconds:
“You forgot our minute.”
Hold the camera completely still.
At 55 seconds, tilt down.
A thin strip of warm light appears beneath the wall, as if a real doorway exists on its other side.
No music. Just ordinary room tone from beyond it.
58–72 SECONDS — THE FLIP
Camera moves closer to the plain wall.
At 60 seconds, The Algorithm:
“That wasn’t in the recording.”
At 64 seconds, cut to the fake bedside chair.
Its seat is covered in dust.
The book has no other pages.
The night-light’s cord leads into the wall-mounted camera.
At 68 seconds, cut back to the strip of light.
The Algorithm:
“Someone noticed you were gone.”
The wall develops the outline of an ordinary door through a restrained lighting and geometry transition. No glowing portal, magical particles, or explosive effect.
72–87 SECONDS — THE PRICE OF LEAVING
Camera approaches the new door.
At 74 seconds, the station lights switch from warm amber to dirty teal.
Text appears on the camera’s tally display:
“LEAVING WILL END YOUR SESSION.”
At 78 seconds:
“YOUR AUDIENCE IS STILL HERE.”
Camera briefly turns back toward the hallway.
It now leads into the transmission chamber from the previous film. Suspended CRTs watch from the darkness.
Every screen displays the same exact word:
“STAY.”
At 82 seconds, The Algorithm:
“They can keep watching.”
At 85 seconds:
“You can go.”
Camera turns decisively toward the door.
No frantic movement. The choice is calm.
87–101 SECONDS — THE HANDLE
Close first-person composition of the door handle.
An adult hand enters frame if a convincing existing asset is available. Otherwise, frame the handle closely enough that its movement can imply the action.
At 89 seconds, the handle begins to turn.
The station’s screens become audible behind us through overlapping relay clicks, not voices.
At 92 seconds, all screens fall silent.
One final caption from the station:
“WHO WILL YOU BE WITHOUT US?”
Hold for two seconds.
At 96 seconds, the door opens.
From the other side:
“Dad.”
Let that one word answer the question.
The light beyond is ordinary bedside lamplight. Keep exposure controlled. No blinding white transition.
101–114 SECONDS — OUR MINUTE
Cut into the real child’s room.
Match the broad arrangement of the false room, but replace its staged perfection with specific signs of use:

* A bent book cover.
* A toy half beneath the bed.
* An uneven blanket.
* A chair angled toward the pillow.
* A glass of water on a coaster.

No camera above the bed.
No tally lights.
No screens.
Frame from the father’s perspective.
At 103 seconds, move toward the chair.
At 106 seconds, lower the camera naturally as he sits.
A small hand emerges from the blanket and rests near his hand at the edge of frame. Use a simple, carefully posed asset or a partial silhouette. Avoid distracting anatomy.
Exact dialogue caption at 108 seconds:
“Just a minute?”
Exact reply at 111 seconds:
“I’m here.”
Do not add narration explaining the lesson.
114–120 SECONDS — STILL HERE
Hold the bedside composition until 116 seconds.
The blanket moves slightly with breathing.
The house settles.
A quiet page turns.
At 116 seconds, cut to black.
Exact title:
“THE STOOPID SHOW”
Below:
“STILL HERE”
At 118 seconds, add:
“END.”
Keep the ordinary room tone underneath until 120 seconds.
End completely.
No autoplay message.
No final monster.
No hidden red light.
The family scene must remain real. Do not invalidate the emotional resolution with another trap.
VISUAL RULES
Give false home and real home different photographic treatment.
FALSE HOME:

* Symmetrical framing.
* Mechanically smooth camera moves.
* Warm light contaminated by teal shadows.
* Repeated props.
* Subtle hold-melody undertone.
* Everything positioned as if awaiting a commercial shoot.

REAL HOME:

* Slightly imperfect composition.
* Gentle bodily camera movement.
* Soft, ordinary lamplight.
* Specific wear on objects.
* No film glitch effects.
* No sentimental slow motion.
* No exaggerated golden glow.

The final scene should feel smaller than the station, yet more substantial.
Use existing family photographs only if explicitly supplied for this project. Do not fabricate recognizable likenesses of Dev’s family.
SOUND
Carry continuity through the relay click and four-note hold melody.
Distinguish the real family signal through its simplicity:

* Three knocks.
* A voice or stable dialogue caption.
* Small room ambience.
* Quiet breathing.
* A page turning.

Avoid an emotional orchestral swell. Trust the bedside scene.
If voice files are unavailable, implement the complete film with timed dialogue captions and clearly organized optional audio slots. Do not pretend voice assets exist.
TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS
Reuse the existing JavaScript/Three.js project where practical.
Use one deterministic 120-second timeline.
All camera movement, room transformations, captions, light cues, and sound events must derive from timeline time. Arbitrary seeking must reconstruct the correct state.
Preserve working:

* Play/pause.
* Restart.
* Seek.
* Mute.
* Fullscreen.
* Quality settings.
* Reduced-motion and reduced-flicker options.
* Clean capture mode.

Ensure exact on-screen wording is legible at 1080p and visible in video capture. Composite story captions into the captured picture.
The transition from false hallway to transmission chamber must preserve perspective. Use a motivated cut or occlusion if a seamless transformation would look unreliable.
Bundle assets locally. Do not require paid services or API keys.
Prioritize the actual film over adding interface features.
